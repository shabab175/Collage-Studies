# Block 2 — Unit 1: Plays - Hamlet and Twelfth Night — Easy Notes

These simplified notes follow the supplied course material. Everyday examples are teaching comparisons; critical interpretations are not automatically facts or the author’s personal beliefs.

## The chapter in one sentence
Hamlet turns uncertain knowledge into tragic action, while Twelfth Night turns mistaken identity into a comedy with a troubling underside.

## Start with a picture
In one theatre, a prince asks whether he can trust what he has been told. In another, a shipwrecked woman changes her outward identity to survive. Both stories make characters act on incomplete information, but their outcomes differ.

## 1. Hamlet: people and situation
Hamlet’s father is dead. His uncle Claudius has become king and married Gertrude, Hamlet’s mother. A Ghost claiming to be the dead king says Claudius murdered him and demands revenge. Horatio is Hamlet’s trusted friend; Polonius advises the court; Ophelia and her brother Laertes are Polonius’s children.

### Follow the five-act movement
- **Act I:** the court’s public order contrasts with Hamlet’s grief; the Ghost introduces the accusation.
- **Act II:** surveillance increases. Hamlet adopts an unusual manner and encounters travelling players.
- **Act III:** the staged murder tests Claudius’s response. Hamlet confronts Gertrude and kills the concealed Polonius.
- **Act IV:** Hamlet is sent towards England; Ophelia’s distress deepens and she dies. Laertes returns seeking revenge.
- **Act V:** graveyard reflection leads towards the poisoned duel. Gertrude, Laertes, Claudius, and Hamlet die; Fortinbras arrives.

Exact divisions and readings vary between editions. Follow the course text when quoting.

### Why the soliloquies matter
Early speeches express grief and disgust. The speech prompted by the players questions Hamlet’s own inaction. “To be or not to be” considers existence, suffering, and the fear surrounding death. Later moments show changing attitudes to action and providence. Do not reduce every speech to the same complaint.

### Madness and performance
Hamlet announces an intention to perform an “antic disposition,” but that does not make every feeling false. Ophelia’s distress is not simply the same performance in another character. Her songs expose grief and pressures the court has not adequately heard. Avoid using a modern diagnosis as a substitute for analysis.

### Main questions
What counts as proof? Can revenge restore justice? How does spying damage trust? What happens when thought and violence fail to find a stable relation? The play within the play, the Ghost, and the final duel supply different forms of evidence and action.

## 2. Twelfth Night: people and situation
Viola survives a shipwreck and believes her twin Sebastian is lost. Disguised as Cesario, she enters Duke Orsino’s service. Orsino loves Olivia, but Olivia falls for Cesario, while Viola loves Orsino. The audience can understand the triangle more fully than the characters.

### Follow the main movement
The shipwreck establishes separation. Viola’s disguise creates opportunity and emotional difficulty. Sir Toby, Maria, and their companions deceive Olivia’s steward Malvolio with a forged letter. Sebastian arrives, creating mistaken encounters. Recognition distinguishes the twins and rearranges relationships, while Malvolio leaves angrily.

### Why Malvolio matters
His ambition and self-importance make him vulnerable to the trick. Yet his confinement and treatment also reveal cruelty. The label “mad” can be used by other people to control someone; it should not be accepted as an objective medical judgement.

### Other figures and unresolved edges
Feste’s songs and wordplay test other people’s claims. Antonio’s loyalty to Sebastian introduces attachment and exclusion that marriage-based closure does not fully settle. Sir Andrew’s aspirations and Sir Toby’s behaviour complicate the festive atmosphere.

## 3. Compare the plays
Both use performance, misunderstanding, desire, and unequal knowledge. Hamlet intensifies uncertainty towards irreversible destruction. Twelfth Night turns the twin confusion towards recognition but does not remove every injury.

## Quick check
1. Why stage a murder in Hamlet? **To test Claudius’s response to a representation of the alleged crime.**
2. Who knows Cesario is Viola? **The audience does, while several central characters do not.**
3. Is Malvolio’s final anger irrelevant to comedy? **No; it exposes the cost of the festive plot.**

## Build an exam answer

1. Establish the shared problem of appearance and knowledge.
2. Analyse the Ghost, staged murder, and soliloquy.
3. Explain Viola’s disguise and the love triangle.
4. Use Malvolio to qualify the comic contrast.
5. Compare the endings and the cost of misinterpretation.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not call all of Hamlet’s emotion feigned, assume Ophelia’s precise state is clinically established, or describe Twelfth Night as happy for everyone.
