# Block 1 — Unit 3: Major Classifications of Shakespearean Plays — Easy Notes

[Full textbook unit](../study%20materials/Block%2001%20-%20Unit%2003%20-%20Major%20Classifications%20of%20Shakespearean%20Plays.md) · PDF pages 57–74.

These simplified notes follow the supplied course material. Everyday examples are teaching comparisons; critical interpretations are not automatically facts or the author’s personal beliefs.

## The chapter in one sentence
Genres help us recognise a play’s patterns, while social discourses help us ask what its conflicts mean.

## Start with a picture
A bookshop has shelves labelled mystery, comedy, and history. A single book may belong on more than one shelf. Shakespeare’s genre labels work like these signs: helpful guides, not locked boxes.

## 1. Tragedy
Tragedies involve grave conflict, suffering, and loss. Hamlet and Macbeth give obvious examples, but their paths to catastrophe differ. “A great man with one fatal flaw” is too narrow a definition. Choices, institutions, mistakes, and chance can all matter.

**Pity** and **fear** describe important responses associated with tragedy. Explain why an outcome matters instead of identifying tragedy only by a body count.

## 2. Comedy
Comedies often use disguise, misrecognition, witty dialogue, festive settings, and marriages or reunions. Twelfth Night resolves the twin confusion, but Malvolio’s treatment leaves discomfort. An ending can establish a new arrangement without making every character happy.

## 3. History and Roman plays
The English histories explore kingship, succession, national identity, and legitimacy. Richard II, the Henry IV plays, and Richard III offer different treatments. They are dramatic interpretations of history, not neutral transcripts.

“Roman plays” is a grouping by setting and sources. Julius Caesar, Antony and Cleopatra, and Coriolanus are also tragedies. These two labels can apply at different levels.

## 4. Romance
Later plays often grouped as romances combine separation, danger, wonder, recognition, forgiveness, and reunion. The Tempest and The Winter’s Tale are major examples. Romance here does not simply mean a love story, and the grouping was developed by later criticism.

## 5. Problem plays
Measure for Measure and All’s Well That Ends Well are often called problem plays because their moral situations and resolutions resist easy satisfaction. The category is debated. An uneasy ending is a reason to examine a classification rather than repeat it mechanically.

## 6. Shakespeare and English
His writing exploits word formation, flexible grammar, multiple registers, punning, and figurative expression. Many familiar expressions have early recorded appearances in the plays. “First surviving record” does not prove that one writer invented a word nobody had previously spoken.

## 7. Social discourses: the arguments a society lives with
**Humanism** asks about education and human capacities. **Feudalism** concerns hierarchies of land, obligation, and service. **Patriarchy** gives men institutional authority over women. **Imperialism** concerns the expansion and domination of power across peoples and territories.

Questions of same-sex desire and gendered identity also appear. Modern categories such as “homosexual” do not map neatly onto every early modern relationship. Analyse the language and historical setting without denying the presence of desire or assuming a proven biography.

## A simple way to apply the ideas
For The Tempest, begin with romance and reconciliation. Then ask who controls the island, whose labour makes events possible, and what freedom means for different figures. Genre and criticism now work together.

## Quick check
1. Can a Roman play also be a tragedy? **Yes; setting and genre are different classifications.**
2. Does a comic ending guarantee universal happiness? **No.**
3. Does a dictionary’s first example prove invention? **No; earlier uses may not survive.**

## Build an exam answer

1. Define genre and explain its usefulness.
2. Distinguish setting labels from formal patterns.
3. Analyse one apparently comic or romantic resolution.
4. Introduce a social question the label cannot settle.
5. Conclude that classification should lead to close reading.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not treat all plays as perfectly fitted to exclusive categories or claim that every printed word first attested in Shakespeare was invented by him.
