These are simplified learning notes based on the supplied course material. Everyday comparisons are teaching examples, not quotations or events from the textbook.

## The chapter in one sentence
Kyd shows revenge growing from failed justice, while Jonson uses comedy to expose people trapped by jealousy, vanity, and mistaken beliefs.

## Start with a picture
One story asks, “What happens when a grieving father cannot obtain justice?” The other asks, “What happens when people believe every foolish suspicion in their heads?” Both plays connect individual behaviour with social order, but their emotional effects differ.

## 1. Thomas Kyd: The Spanish Tragedy
### Meet the main figures
- **Don Andrea:** a dead Spanish soldier whose ghost watches events.
- **Revenge:** a personified force accompanying the ghost.
- **Bel-Imperia:** Andrea’s former beloved, later in love with Horatio.
- **Horatio:** son of Hieronimo and Isabella.
- **Hieronimo:** a royal judicial officer and grieving father.
- **Lorenzo:** Bel-Imperia’s brother, who helps plan Horatio’s murder.
- **Balthazar:** a Portuguese prince interested in Bel-Imperia.

### Follow the main events
Andrea has died in war. His ghost and Revenge observe the unfolding action. Bel-Imperia turns towards Horatio, but Lorenzo and Balthazar arrange Horatio’s murder. Hieronimo discovers his son’s body and seeks an explanation and justice.

Powerful people obstruct the search. Bel-Imperia’s message helps expose responsibility. Hieronimo eventually organises a court entertainment, a **play within the play**, which becomes the means of actual killing. Revenge produces further deaths, including those of the revengers themselves.

### What is the play asking?
**Justice versus revenge:** the law should protect people, but unequal power prevents it from doing so. Hieronimo’s suffering makes his anger understandable without making every action morally simple.

**Grief:** revenge begins in an intimate loss, not merely in a wish for excitement.

**Theatre and reality:** a staged performance becomes real violence. The audience must think about the power and danger of representation.

**Fate:** the ghost and Revenge frame events within forces beyond the human characters’ full control.

## 2. Ben Jonson: Every Man in His Humour
### What does “humour” mean?
It means a dominant disposition associated with an old theory of bodily fluids. A person can be so governed by one tendency that it distorts ordinary judgement.

Imagine wearing jealous spectacles: even an innocent conversation begins to look suspicious. The spectacles represent the controlling humour.

### Follow the main situation
In the English version used in this unit, Knowell worries about his son Edward’s companions and interests. Brainworm uses disguises and deception. Kitely is excessively jealous about his wife. Stephen and Matthew want to appear fashionable or accomplished, while Bobadill boasts of military courage.

Misunderstandings multiply because people trust their own vanity or fear. Edward and Bridget’s relationship develops amid these confusions. Justice Clement helps expose errors and restore order.

### What should you notice?
- **Kitely:** jealousy makes him misread situations.
- **Bobadill:** boasting and actual courage do not match.
- **Stephen and Matthew:** pretension replaces genuine understanding.
- **Brainworm:** disguise and trickery move the plot.
- **Knowell:** parental anxiety becomes an object of examination.

Jonson’s satire encourages an audience to recognise social follies in familiar life.

## Compare the plays
| Question | The Spanish Tragedy | Every Man in His Humour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main pressure | Murder and blocked justice | Distorted judgement and pretension |
| Emotional movement | Grief towards destructive revenge | Confusion towards exposure |
| Major device | Ghost and play within a play | Disguise and comic misunderstanding |
| Social question | Can power prevent justice? | Can people recognise their own folly? |

## Build an exam answer
For revenge tragedy, connect the ghost, murder, failed justice, delay, and final performance. For comedy of humours, explain the concept and then show how two characters act under a dominant disposition.

## Quick check
1. Who is Horatio’s father? **Hieronimo.**
2. What turns performance into a weapon? **The final play within the play.**
3. Is a “humour” simply a joke? **No; it is a governing disposition.**
