- Clearly articulate your main argument in a concise manner at the beginning of your answer.
- This sets the tone for your analysis and helps the reader understand the central point you are trying to convey.
- Support your analysis with specific examples and quotes from the literature. Use direct textual evidence to illustrate and reinforce your points.

## Questions — choose any one

1. Examine the “General Prologue” to The Canterbury Tales as a portrait gallery of fourteenth-century English society

2. Analyse Edmund Spenser’s “Prothalamion” as a marriage poem, with particular attention to its celebration of love, marriage, and harmony.

3. Analyse Francis Bacon’s treatment of human relationships and social conduct in “Of Friendship” and “Of Truth.”

4. Analyse Sir Thomas More’s Utopia as a critique of contemporary English society and an exploration of an ideal social and political order.

5. Analyse Thomas Kyd’s The Spanish Tragedy as a revenge play, with particular attention to its conventions, themes, and treatment of revenge.
