# Coverage review — Chaucer to the Elizabethan Age

## Result

All 15 textbook units and their prescribed literary works are represented. The earlier exam PDFs were useful summaries but did omit supporting details relevant to several assignment questions. Every PDF has now been supplemented. The reviewed set contains 71 pages, with each unit between 4 and 6 pages, compared with 282 pages in the supplied textbook.

This is a review of unit, topic, and assignment-prompt coverage, with selected factual and primary-text checks. It is not a claim that every textbook sentence, footnote, quotation, biography, or possible exam answer has been reproduced or independently verified.

## What was checked

- The original PDF has 282 pages. Its extracted study-material files allocate PDF pages 1–282 once each, without a missing or duplicate page marker. This verifies page allocation, not perfect OCR or image extraction.
- All 15 units have a simple Markdown note, a reviewed expanded PDF, and a sticky-note image.
- All prescribed poems, plays, and essays appearing in those units are named and explained in the notes. Broader historical surveys remain selective rather than mini-biographies of every author.
- Unit headings, overviews, and assignment prompts were compared with the earlier PDF content. Thin areas were supplemented as recorded below.
- All 101 numbered unit assignment prompts are now included in the PDF revision checklists. A checklist is not a set of 101 fully written answers or a prediction of exam questions. Obvious line-order and broken-word artifacts were repaired in the checklist, while the extracted textbook files were left unchanged.
- Every regenerated PDF was reopened and its extracted text checked for the supplementary section and question checklist. A long-title chapter was also rendered for layout inspection.

## Specific factual and source checks

1. **Jonson and Cob:** the Anglicized text shows Bobadill beating Cob after Cob attacks tobacco in Act III, scene II. Cob explains the reason to Justice Clement in Act III, scene III. The course summary’s claim concerning Cob’s wife should not be used as the answer to this incident. The reviewed PDF supplies the primary-text explanation.
2. **Priests in Utopia:** Book II explains their small numbers, election, high public honour, duties in worship and moral education, and exceptional status. This fills a gap between the course’s short account and its assignment question. The PDF also retains the contradictions of the island’s institutions.
3. **Herbert:** his death and The Temple’s posthumous publication belong to 1633. The supplied material’s suggestion that he died the previous year should not be repeated.
4. **Bacon’s Caesar example:** the account of Caesar’s special favour concerns Decimus Brutus. Avoid silently identifying him with Marcus Brutus.
5. **Editions matter:** Doctor Faustus has A- and B-text differences; Jonson’s play has versions with different settings and names. Use the assigned edition for exact wording and scene references.

## What you should still study in the textbook

- Exact quotations, passage annotations, and close reading of the prescribed texts. The new prose is explanatory paraphrase.
- Full act-by-act or stanza-by-stanza treatment where required by a question.
- Writer biographies and additional works when the question specifically asks for them.
- The separate self-assessment answers and model question papers in `study materials/`; they remain separate from the 15 chapter PDFs.
- Original page images, diagrams, typography, and especially the visual layout of Easter Wings. A text extract cannot replace the source PDF for these features.

The sticky-note images are deliberately selective visual memory aids. Their short captions have been checked for the central concepts; drawings are illustrative, not historical evidence.
