# Block 1 — Unit 4: Prose - Historical Development — Easy Notes

[Full textbook unit](../study%20materials/Block%2001%20-%20Unit%2004%20-%20Prose%20-%20Historical%20Development.md) · PDF pages 45–59.

These are simplified teaching notes. Everyday examples are explanatory comparisons; critical readings and theoretical models are not automatically established facts.

## The chapter in one sentence
Prose expands through political argument, spiritual narrative, diaries, periodical essays, satire, criticism, and new forms of extended fiction.

## Start with a picture
A writing desk holds a pamphlet, a private diary, a weekly essay, a letter, and a novel. Each addresses a different situation, but techniques can move between them.

## 1. Argument and spiritual narrative
Milton’s prose intervenes in religious and political controversies, including the licensing debate in Areopagitica. Its argument is historically situated; do not assume a seventeenth-century defence of unlicensed printing means identical rights for every group in the modern sense.

Bunyan’s The Pilgrim’s Progress uses allegory to make a spiritual journey visible. Names and events can have symbolic meanings while also creating memorable narrative action.

## 2. Diaries
Samuel Pepys and John Evelyn record personal and public experience through particular social positions. A diary’s immediate detail can be valuable, but its writer selects and interprets events. Private writing is not automatically an objective historical record.

## 3. Periodical essays
Addison, Steele, and later Johnson address readers through repeated publication. Character sketches, observation, irony, and moral reflection turn everyday conduct into discussion. A returning persona or fictional club can create continuity across separate essays.

The medium helps establish a reading habit and a public voice, although access to that public remains unequal.

## 4. Satire and criticism
Swift uses narrative and comparison to question intellectual pride and institutional claims. Dryden, Pope, and Johnson develop influential critical arguments in different forms. Johnson’s Dictionary and Lives of the Poets contribute to language and literary judgement, while his essays examine conduct and self-deception.

Burke’s political prose adds rhetoric about institutions, history, change, and public feeling. Do not classify every argumentative writer as holding one Enlightenment position.

## 5. The novel’s development
Defoe draws on travel, providential narrative, and detailed accounts. Richardson develops letters and inward experience. Fielding uses an overt narrator and comic organisation; Smollett and Sterne expand satire, movement, and formal experiment.

Women writers, including Behn, Haywood, and Burney, are important to this history. A story of three male inventors would erase much of the field.

## 6. What to compare
Ask about purpose, readership, speaker, structure, and style. A compressed aphorism, a descriptive scene, and a sustained argument guide readers differently. Prose is a flexible range of methods, not simply language without rhyme.

## Quick check
1. Is allegory only an abstract lecture? **No; it represents ideas through narrative.**
2. Is a diary perfectly neutral? **No.**
3. Did one writer invent every aspect of the novel? **No.**

## Build an exam answer

1. Group prose by purpose and situation.
2. Explain argument, diary, and allegory.
3. Discuss the recurring reader of periodicals.
4. Connect older practices with novelistic methods.
5. Include stylistic and gender diversity.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not describe prose history as a single style or exclude earlier and women’s fiction from the rise of the novel.
