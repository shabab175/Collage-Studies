Background
   The seventeenth century was a time of prose. The language of the era was
 characterised by an interest in scientific details and unhurried observation.
 Clarity, directness, and economy of words were stressed in this new kind of
 writing. It initially appeared in the Essays of Bacon sometime before 1600.
    At the same time, the optimism of the 18th century was its most
 noticeable trait. It was a moment when the superiority of the human mind
 was honoured. All of creation was thought to be subject to examination.
 Even the period’s descriptive historical titles of the period convey a mood
 of advancement and betterment. Many individuals at the time believed that
 they were living in a golden age like that of Augustus, the Roman emperor.
 This is why the early 18th century was referred to as “Augustan.” During
 the period, traders and merchants attained tremendous economic power.
 Discoveries in science were promoted. An industrial society was ushered
 in by numerous significant discoveries, including the spinning jenny, the
 power loom, and the steam engine. Cities expanded in size, and London
 started to take on its current role as a major industrial and commercial hub.
 The middle class desired a respectable and moralistic art that was governed
 by commonsense in addition to a pleasant living. They objected in outrage
 to the immorality of the aristocracy prevalent in much of the Restoration
 literature.
