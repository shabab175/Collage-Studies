Background
   The Puritan Age, or the Age of Milton (1600-1660), is further divided into the
Jacobean and Caroline periods, named after the rulers James I and Charles I
and the Restoration Period, or the Age of Dryden (1660-1700). After the rise
of the Puritan movement in England in the 17th century, the Puritan age was
named so. The Puritans were a group of English-speaking Protestants who
were dissatisfied with the religious reformation movement that took place
during Elizabeth’s reign. They advocated the complete purification of the
Church of England and pure Christianity (thus, the name Puritan). During
the English Civil War (1642-1651), when the people were divided into two
factions, one loyal to the King and the other opposed to him, the Puritans
emerged as a powerful political force. The crisis began when James I, who
had inherited the right to rule as a result of an Act of Parliament, placed an
excessive emphasis on the Divine Right and began to ignore the Parliament
that had created him. The movement for constitutional reforms was heralded
by the Puritans, who had become a powerful force in the social life of the
time. The English Civil War was a series of battles between Charles I and his
supporters, known as the Cavaliers, and the Parliamentarians, also known as
the Roundheads. He was asked by Parliament to sign a petition of rights, but
he refused, demonstrating open contempt for Parliament and the people. He
also had failed foreign expeditions to France and other places. Following the
