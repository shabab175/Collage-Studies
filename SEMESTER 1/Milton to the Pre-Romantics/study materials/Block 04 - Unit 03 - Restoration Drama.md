Background
    Despite a Parliamentary restriction on dramatic performances in 1642,
  the theatre continued to play a role in English culture throughout the
  English Civil Wars and the succeeding Commonwealth. Between 1640 and
  1660, English Puritans tried to reform numerous aspects of English politics,
  administration, and society. Elizabeth I, James I, and Charles I had all
  publicly supported the theatre and had opposed Puritan endeavors to rid
  the country of theatre, despite a Parliamentary resolution that outlawed it in
  1642. Since the majority of London’s performers, playwrights, and theatre-
  owners supported the royalist cause during the Civil War, many of them
  were sent into exile when their side lost.




Keywords

 Restoration Play, Comedy of Manners, Immorality

  From around 1660 to 1700, there was a flourishing period for restoration
comedy, composed and performed following the restoration of the Stuart
monarchy. The people, including King Charles, revolted against the Puritan
values in opposition to Cromwell’s rule. The Restoration plays, which seem
