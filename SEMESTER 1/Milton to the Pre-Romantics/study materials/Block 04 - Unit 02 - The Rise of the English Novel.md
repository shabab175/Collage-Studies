Background
    The English novel took its refined form and pattern in the 18th century,
 though some raw forms of it existed in other languages. Increased release
 of novels could be seen between 1685 and 1815.The novel is regularly and
 profoundly connected with the society in which the novelist lives and feels
 and they explain, glorify, or critique more than any other literary form. The
 social, political, economic, scientific, and cultural facets of England have
 had a significant impact on the English novel throughout its history, from its
 diverse origins to its evolution in the eighteenth century through its rise in
 the nineteenth century to its current status.

Summarised Overview

   The novel from the 18th century was deemed realistic because its characters
were realistic individuals with common names and titles, were described in
the course of their daily lives, were situated in real places, and were based on
real events. Contrary to the early Augustans, novelists preferred to represent
ordinary folk behaving in realistic situations. The novelists tried their best to
connect to the middle-class readers who sought to relate with the protagonists
of the stories and desired to read about ordinary folks. The most significant
novelists of the time were Daniel Defoe, Samuel Richardson and Henry Fielding.
