Background

     With its realistic subjects and real-life autobiographies of the major
  characters, the novel form of writing has entertained readers as one of the
  great literary forms in English literature. The English novel has been a major
  component of English literature for a very long time. Daniel Defoe, Samuel
  Richardson, Henry Fielding, Laurence Sterne, Oliver Goldsmith, Tobias
  Smollett, Charles Dickens, and others are some of the novelists in English
  literature. The early English books Robinson Crusoe by Daniel Defoe and
  Tom Jones by Henry Fielding are credited with establishing the novel as a
  genre at the start of the eighteenth century. The industrial revolution and
  the emergence of the middle class increased people’s desire to read about
  topics relevant to their everyday experiences. Daniel Defoe, who made
  significant contributions to English literature, particularly in the area of
  novel writing, is credited with helping the English novel flourish. He later
  came to be known as the “father of the English novel.”

Background

    Pamela or Virtue Rewarded is an epistolary novel by Samuel Richardson,
 first published in 1740. It tells the story of a maid named Pamela whose
 master, Mr B., makes unwanted advances towards her. She rejects him
 continually, and her virtue is eventually rewarded when he shows his
 sincerity by proposing an equitable marriage to her. In the second part of
 the novel, Pamela attempts to accommodate herself to upper-class society
 and to build a successful relationship with him. The story was widely
 mocked at the time for its perceived licentiousness and it inspired Henry
 Fielding (among many others) to write two parodies: Shamela (1741), about
 Pamela’s true identity; and Joseph Andrews (1742), about Pamela’s brother.
    Pamela was Richardson’s first novel, and according to the critics it was
 the first novel in English literature since Defoe’s works were more or less
 accounts of accidental adventures. Richardson must have been upset by
 the prevailing evil of exploitation of the poor young maids by the rich.
 Richardson chose epistolary form to enable the young maid-servants to give
 a daily account of the treatment they were getting from the rich employers.

Summarised Overview

     One of the most well-known love stories from the eighteenth century is
  found in Samuel Richardson’s Pamela: or Virtue Rewarded, even if the book
  may not sound particularly romantic to readers today. In it, Pamela, a lovely
  and virginal serving girl, is aggressively pursued by a wealthy man named
  Mr. B for her virginity. Mr. B is a sleazy rake who wants nothing more than
  to get his way with the young girl. He makes repeated attempts to rape and
  attack her. Pamela, who is a model of morality and consistently manages to
  evade Mr. B’s attacks, is unfortunate for him. Eventually, Pamela’s inherent
  kindness transforms Mr. B, and he stops pursuing her and instead proposes
  to her. Pamela eventually decides to accept Mr. B’s proposal and becomes
  engaged in an unexpected turn.
