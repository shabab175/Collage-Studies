 Background
    A mock-epic is a short narrative poem that uses the devices and conventions
 of the epic to treat unimportant subjects, turning it into a parody or burlesque
 of the epic. The use of the grandiloquent epic-style and epic-conventions
 for a theme that is essentially trivial and insignificant results in a mocking,
 ridiculous effect. The ancient Mock-epic The Battle of the Frog and Mice, a
 parody of Homer’s Iliad, Jonathan Swift’s Tale of a Tub and Battle of the Books,
 and Alexander Pope’s Dunciad and The Rape of the Lock, Mac Flecknoe by John
 Dryden are the finest examples of the mock-epic.
