Background

        The seventeenth and eighteenth centuries were transformative periods in
     British history, marked by political upheaval, religious conflict, and profound
     intellectual and cultural change. From the early Stuart monarchy and the
     English Civil War to the Glorious Revolution and the rise of constitutional
     government, this era witnessed the gradual decline of absolute monarchy
     and the emergence of parliamentary democracy. Simultaneously, the
     Enlightenment reshaped ideas about reason, individual rights, and the role
     of government, influencing both literature and society. The Restoration of
     the monarchy, the formation of Great Britain, and the impact of the French
     Revolution further accelerated these shifts. This unit explores the key
     historical events, ideological movements, and literary developments that
     defined this dynamic period and laid the foundation for the modern British
     state and its cultural identity.


Keywords
English Civil Wars, Restoration, Enlightenment, French Revolution

Summarised Overview
   The unit traces the sweeping political, cultural, and intellectual
transformations of Britain from the early 1600s through the Enlightenment
era. It begins with James I’s unification of the crowns and ends with the birth
of modern constitutional government. It explores the reigns of key monarchs-
James I, Charles I, Charles II, James II and the revolutionary developments
they incited, culminating in the Glorious Revolution and the establishment
of Parliamentary supremacy under William and Mary. The Enlightenment
period witnessed a new valuation of reason, individual liberty, and empirical
inquiry, which profoundly influenced literature, governance, and society. By
the end of the 18th century, the effects of the French Revolution further altered
British consciousness, shifting literary and cultural focus from rationalism to




emotion and introspection.

1. Discuss the concept of the Divine Right of Kings and its political conse-
   quences during the Stuart reigns.
2. Evaluate the role of the English Civil War in shaping the relationship
   between the monarchy and Parliament.
3. Analyse the cultural and literary impact of the Restoration period, with
   special reference to Restoration comedy.
4. How did the Enlightenment challenge traditional religious and political
   authority in 18th-century Britain?
5. Examine the influence of the French Revolution on the transition from
   the Enlightenment to Romanticism in British literature.
