Background
   After a short revolutionary period of the Puritans, the parliamentary rule,
the restoration period of British history began with the return of the exiled
Stuart King, King Charles II to the English throne. Secular entertainment
was frowned upon and despised while the parliament was in power, and the
majority of theatres were shut down. However, when King Charles returned
from France, King carried with him a love of entertainment and leisure,
two interests shared by the upper-class of the society. Scholars and artists
throughout the Restoration Period were fascinated with ‘reason’ because they
thought it was what set humans apart from other creatures. Many artists used
satire to recognise human reason, identifying a social issue and examining it
through the perspective of satirical humour and, frequently, ridicule.

                       2.4.4 Satire, Mock-Epic, and Comedy of Manners
                          William Congreve’s The Way of the World (1700) is
                       widely acknowledged as the crowning achievement of
                       Restoration comedy. It reflects the age’s obsession with wit,
                       fashion, intrigue, and above all, the relations between men
                       and women in love and marriage. While the Restoration
                       stage is notorious for libertinism and satire, Congreve’s
                       play marks a refinement of the genre, combining biting
                       commentary on false relationships with a serious concern
                       for what constitutes an ideal marriage. It is simultaneously
                       a mock-epic that ridicules trivial pursuits with heroic
                       language and a quintessential Comedy of Manners that
                       mirrors the elite society of post-Restoration England.
                          The Restoration period (1660 onwards) was
                       characterised by loosened morals after the Puritan

Summarised Overview
    This play offers a satirical perspective on the attitude, mannerisms, and
 flaws of the upper classes. The description of the way of the world highlights
 the prevalence of deceit, greed, and love. The plot revolves around two
 lovers, Mirabell and Millamant, seeking a means to marry without losing their
 inheritance. There are several characters who are connected to one another
 in some way. The play also reveals some dark secrets of characters. Mirabell
 and Millamant are keen to get married, but they are conscious that Millamant’s
 aunt, Lady Wishfort, would not approve. They must have her approval to inherit
 her wealth. As a result, it offers an intriguing perspective on the extent the
 couple go to in order to achieve the same purpose by any means.
