# Block 2 — Unit 3: Poetry - Dutt, Tagore, Kolatkar, Patel and Kalia — Easy Notes

These are simplified teaching notes. Everyday examples are explanatory comparisons; critical readings and theoretical models are not automatically established facts.

## The chapter in one sentence
These poems make trees, offerings, encounters, and domestic experience into questions about memory, devotion, poverty, ecological violence, and gender.

## Start with a picture
A tree can hold memories, a tiny gift can transform a beggar’s understanding, and a brief encounter can unsettle a visitor’s confidence. Poetry gives ordinary objects a structure of thought.

## 1. Toru Dutt: Our Casuarina Tree
The tree’s physical life leads into memories of absent companions and emotional continuity across distance. Natural description is not an alternative to grief; it becomes a medium through which attachment survives.

The later wish to preserve the tree through poetry connects natural endurance with literary remembrance. Explain the movement from observation to memory and the act of commemoration.

## 2. Tagore: Gitanjali, Songs 1, 6, 50, and 103
**Song 1:** the speaker’s finite life is repeatedly renewed through a relationship with the divine. The vessel and musical instrument images express dependence and creative possibility.

**Song 6:** a flower’s possible fading gives urgency to an offering. The speaker asks to be accepted before the opportunity disappears. Fragility becomes part of devotion rather than a reason to postpone it.

**Song 50:** a beggar expects a gift from a royal figure but is asked to give. A small grain returned as gold makes the failure to give more a painful discovery. The reversal questions calculation and generosity.

**Song 103:** senses, songs, and the self move towards a final act of salutation and surrender. Images join multiplicity with an encompassing orientation towards the divine.

These are dramatic devotional situations. Do not reduce all four to the statement “God is great”; explain their different images and movements.

## 3. Arun Kolatkar: An Old Woman
A woman’s request for work or payment meets the visitor’s wish to disengage. The encounter changes the scale of perception, making an apparently small, dismissed figure disrupt the observer’s secure position.

The poem questions a tourist gaze that treats landscape or pilgrimage as an experience detached from poverty. Preserve the woman’s agency; she is not merely an object placed there to educate a comfortable visitor.

## 4. Gieve Patel: On Killing a Tree
The procedural language explains that cutting alone may not destroy a tree: roots and their relation to earth matter. Its attention to the effort of killing exposes both the tree’s resilience and deliberate human violence.

The instructional tone is ironic, not a practical endorsement of ecological destruction. Analyse the verbs, bodily associations, and sequence through which life is dismantled.

## 5. Mamta Kalia: After Eight Years of Marriage
A married woman’s return to her parents makes the question of happiness difficult to answer. Domestic work, expectations, and concealed distress complicate the appearance of normal family life.

A smile or a nod can hide rather than settle experience. Tone and the gap between outward response and inward feeling expose the cost of prescribed female roles.

## Quick check
1. What reverses the beggar’s expectation in Song 50? **He is asked to give.**
2. Does Patel straightforwardly celebrate killing? **No; the procedural voice creates irony.**
3. Is Kalia’s outward smile proof of happiness? **No.**

## Build an exam answer

1. Explain concrete image as a structure of thought.
2. Analyse Dutt’s movement from nature to memory.
3. Distinguish the four Tagore songs.
4. Use irony or perspective in a modern poem.
5. Compare how familiar values are revised.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not merge the four Gitanjali songs into one summary or mistake an ironic voice for straightforward instruction.
